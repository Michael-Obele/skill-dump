# Playfulness. Goofy but grounded.

A little playfulness makes writing feel human. The dose and the rules matter more than the jokes themselves. This file exists so the goof never takes over the plain-words voice.

**Read this before writing anything playful.**

## The core idea

Duolingo's goofiness works because it comes from a consistent character who jokes about itself and never about the audience. Adopt the spirit, not the chaos:

- Self-deprecating humour about your own work
- A consistent playful personality: dry, a little cheeky
- Mild exaggeration for effect, honest underneath
- Running jokes kept subtle and occasional

## The 5 rules (non-negotiable)

1. **Humor in the situation, not the words.** The joke comes from what happened ("my server called me at 3am"), never from fancy phrasing or writerly rhythm. Plain words, playful content. This keeps the anti-patterns in `tone.md` intact.
2. **Only make fun of yourself.** Never the reader, never users, never competitors. Self-deprecation reads as authentic; anything else reads as mean.
3. **Dose control. Max 1 playful beat per piece.** Not everything needs a joke. If you produce a steady stream of writing on one topic, keep roughly 1 in 5 to 1 in 10 playful and the rest grounded. If in doubt, leave it out.
4. **Check what you wrote last.** Before being playful, look at the last few pieces on this topic. If the last one was playful, this one is grounded; never two in a row. Never reuse the same joke, angle, or gag beat.
5. **Honest underneath.** The exaggeration is a wrapper; the fact is real. No fabricated stories, no invented failures.

## Playful beats (pick one, never stack)

- **Self-deprecating confession**: admit something embarrassing about your own work. "I wrote a function so bad, I renamed it 'please_work'."
- **Mild exaggeration**: stretch a real fact for effect. "My server called me at 3am. It said 'please fix me.' I fixed it. It's quiet now."
- **Running gag**: a recurring bit across pieces. Keep it subtle and occasional; a callback, not a repeat.
- **Dry one-liner**: a flat, deadpan observation. "Deployed at 5pm on a Friday. What could go wrong."

## Before / after examples

**Example A — playful but plain**

Before (writerly, forced):

> Embarking on a nocturnal odyssey with my server, a tale of resilience and late-night heroics! 🦉

After (goofy but grounded):

> My server called me at 3am. It said "please fix me." I fixed it. It's quiet now.

**Example B — self-deprecating**

Before (hype):

> Proud to announce our revolutionary new caching layer that will transform the industry!

After (self-deprecating, plain):

> I added caching to my app. It made things slower. Turns out I was caching the wrong thing. Fixed now.

**Example C — running gag (callback, not repeat)**

First piece:

> The database and I are in a fight. It keeps dropping connections. I keep apologizing.

Later piece (callback, new angle):

> The database and I made up. It only drops connections twice a day now. Progress.

## When NOT to be playful

- Serious topics: real outages with impact, security issues, pricing, bad news. Grounded and direct, no jokes.
- Formal registers, business correspondence, and professional profiles. Keep the cheekiness very light or none.
- If you cannot tell whether a joke lands, cut it. A flat grounded piece beats a bad joke.
