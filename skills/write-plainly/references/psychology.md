# Psychology of writing. Why these rules work.

The rules in SKILL.md are not arbitrary. Each one maps to a finding about why people read, stop, and trust a voice. Read this when you need the "why" behind a rule, or when a draft feels wrong and you want to diagnose it.

## Attention is the bottleneck

- Most readers see only the first line or two. That is the whole budget you get to earn the read.
- A curiosity gap (signal that something interesting is there without giving it away), surprise, and specificity stop the scroll.
- Openings that evoke emotion, curiosity, or surprise beat neutral openers.

**Practical rule:** the first line must make a specific reader want the second line. If the first line could open any piece ("Excited to announce..."), it is not an opening.

## The imagined reader

- Writers shape text towards an imagined audience, and the text lands differently depending on who that is.
- Psychographics (values, attitudes, motivations) predict engagement better than demographics.
- Writing to "everyone" produces writing that resonates with no one. Writing to one specific person produces writing that resonates with everyone who shares that person's context.

**Practical rule:** define the one reader before writing. If you cannot picture them, the writing will be generic.

## Specificity and credibility

- Concrete details read as true. Vague claims read as filler or advertising.
- Numbers, names, and small specifics are what make a claim checkable by the reader.

**Practical rule:** replace every adjective that carries a claim with a fact. "Much faster" becomes "900ms to 40ms".

## Authenticity and trust

- Perceived authenticity is sincerity plus expertise plus uniqueness plus commitment to values.
- Self-disclosure builds trust and attachment, but performative vulnerability reads as fake and backfires.
- Specific, first person, slightly imperfect writing reads as real. Polished corporate-speak reads as fake.

**Practical rule:** write as the person, not the brand. Honest tradeoffs and real failures build more trust than flawless-sounding claims.

## Emotion

Source: Jonah Berger's research on emotion and virality ("Contagious").

- High-arousal emotions (awe, amusement, anger, anxiety, excitement) increase the urge to pass writing on.
- Low-arousal emotions (sadness, contentment) suppress it.
- The emotional response is the strongest single predictor of whether writing gets shared.

**Practical rule:** before writing, name the emotion you want the reader to feel. If the honest answer is "nothing," the piece will land as nothing. Pick the emotion that fits the content truthfully. Never manufacture drama.

## Repetition, fatigue, and consistency

- Repetitive framing causes reader fatigue and disengagement.
- The same opening, angle, or structure repeated makes readers tune out. Fresh angles earn attention back.
- Consistency (showing up on a rhythm) builds trust. Volume without quality does not.

**Practical rule:** every piece on a topic should open differently from the last one. Vary the opening, the angle, and the format. Prefer fewer, better pieces over more, weaker ones.
